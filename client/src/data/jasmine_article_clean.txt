ABSTRACT

This

study

aims

to

analyze

the

dynamics

of

maritime

security

threats

and

evaluate

the

effectiveness

of

Indonesia's

deterrence

strategy

in

the

North

Natuna

Sea

against

China's

grey-zone

tactics

and

transnational

maritime

crimes.

Using

a

descriptive

qualitative

approach

based

on

library

research,

secondary

data

were

gathered

from

official

government

reports,

the

UNCLOS

1982

legal

framework,

and

academic

literature.

The

findings

reveal

that

Indonesia's

sovereignty

and

sovereign

rights

in

the

North

Natuna

Exclusive

Economic

Zone

(EEZ)

are

continually

challenged

by

coercive

actions

from

the

China

Coast

Guard

(CCG)

and

the

proliferation

of

dark

vessels.

These

external

vulnerabilities

are

exacerbated

by

domestic

structural

issues,

including

jurisdictional

fragmentation

among

seven

ministries/agencies

lacking

a

single

command

system,

operational

inefficiencies

in

patrol

fuel

allocation,

and

a

physical

vacuum

in

the

EEZ

due

to

the

dominance

of

micro-scale

local

fishing

fleets

(<5

GT).

The

study

concludes

that

enhancing

Indonesia's

deterrence

effect

requires

comprehensive

maritime

governance

reform,

focusing

on

establishing

a

unified

command

regulation

under

Bakamla

RI,

integrating

maritime

intelligence

surveillance

technology

(smart

power),

and

mobilizing

local

fishing

fleets

(>30

GT)

as

economic

drivers

and

civilian

early

warning

systems

(soft

power)

along

the

border.
